## 7.1 Die Wohnung

Raj is looking for a flat. He visits one and talks to the landlord, Herr Braun. Listen for the little word **in** and the articles that follow it (*in der Küche*, *im Bad*). They have a new form.

```dialogue
title: Wohnungsbesichtigung
setting: Raj looks at a two-room flat with the landlord.
---
Herr Braun: Guten Tag, Herr Patel. Das ist das Wohnzimmer. | Hello, Mr Patel. This is the living room.
Raj: Es ist hell und groß. Und wo ist die Küche? | It is bright and big. And where is the kitchen?
Herr Braun: Die Küche ist neben dem Wohnzimmer. Dort steht auch der Kühlschrank. | The kitchen is next to the living room. The fridge is also there.
Raj: Gibt es ein Schlafzimmer? | Is there a bedroom?
Herr Braun: Ja, das Schlafzimmer ist hinter der Küche. Im Zimmer gibt es einen Schrank und ein Bett. | Yes, the bedroom is behind the kitchen. In the room there is a wardrobe and a bed.
Raj: Und das Bad? | And the bathroom?
Herr Braun: Das Bad ist im Flur, links neben der Tür. | The bathroom is in the hallway, on the left next to the door.
Raj: Wie hoch ist die Miete? | How high is the rent?
Herr Braun: Sechshundertfünfzig Euro warm. | Six hundred and fifty euros including heating.
```

## 7.2 Rooms and furniture

Most room words are **compound nouns**, and a compound takes the gender of its **last part**:

| Compound | Parts | Gender |
|---|---|---|
| das **Wohn**zimmer | wohnen + das Zimmer | **das** (Zimmer) |
| das **Schlaf**zimmer | schlafen + das Zimmer | **das** |
| das **Bade**zimmer | das Bad + das Zimmer | **das** |
| der **Kühl**schrank | kühl + der Schrank | **der** (Schrank) |
| die **Haus**tür | das Haus + die Tür | **die** (Tür) |

The rooms: **die Küche** (kitchen), **das Bad / das Badezimmer** (bathroom), **das Wohnzimmer** (living room), **das Schlafzimmer** (bedroom), **der Flur** (hall), **der Balkon**, **der Keller** (cellar), **der Garten**. Furniture and things: **der Tisch, der Stuhl, das Sofa, der Schrank, das Regal, das Bett, die Lampe, der Teppich, der Fernseher, das Bild**.

## 7.3 Reading a flat advertisement

Flat ads are full of abbreviations. Read the ad and then decode it with the table.

```dialogue
title: Anzeige
setting: An online advertisement for a flat.
---
Annonce: 2-Zi-Wohnung, 55 m², 3. OG, Balkon, EBK, Bad mit Dusche, ruhige Lage, ab sofort frei. KM 520 €, NK 130 €, Kaution 3 MM. | 2-room flat, 55 square metres, third floor, balcony, fitted kitchen, bathroom with shower, quiet location, available immediately. Cold rent 520 €, extra costs 130 €, deposit three months' rent.
```

| Abbreviation | Means | In English |
|---|---|---|
| Zi. | Zimmer | room |
| m² | Quadratmeter | square metres |
| OG | Obergeschoss | upper floor (1. OG = first floor above the ground floor) |
| EBK | Einbauküche | fitted kitchen |
| KM | Kaltmiete | rent without heating and costs |
| NK | Nebenkosten | extra costs (heating, water, rubbish) |
| Kaution | | deposit |
| MM | Monatsmieten | months' rent |
| WG | Wohngemeinschaft | flat share |

```merke
**Kaltmiete + Nebenkosten = Warmmiete.** The advertised price is often *Kaltmiete*. Always ask what the *Warmmiete* is. That is what you really pay each month. In Germany the **ground floor** is **das Erdgeschoss**, so *das erste Obergeschoss* is the floor above, what English counts as the second floor.
```

## 7.4 Where is it? The dative case

So far you know two cases: the **nominative** (the subject) and the **accusative** (the direct object). To say **where** something is, German uses the **dative** case after position words. You can recognise it at once because the articles change:

| | masculine | feminine | neuter | plural |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| definite nominative | der | die | das | die |
| definite **dative** | **dem** | **der** | **dem** | **den** (+ **-n**) |
| indefinite **dative** | **einem** | **einer** | **einem** | |
| kein / mein dative | keinem, meinem | keiner, meiner | keinem, meinem | keinen, meinen |

Two things to notice. **Masculine and neuter are the same** (*dem, einem*), and **feminine is *der* / *einer***, which looks like the masculine nominative but is not. In the **plural** the noun itself usually takes **-n**: *die Zimmer → in den Zimmern*, *die Kinder → mit den Kindern*. (If the plural already ends in -n or -s, nothing is added: *den Autos*.)

### Two-way prepositions

Nine prepositions can take the accusative or the dative: **an, auf, hinter, in, neben, über, unter, vor, zwischen**. The question decides which:

| Question | Meaning | Case |
|---|---|---|
| **Wo?** (where?) | position, no movement | **dative** |
| **Wohin?** (where to?) | movement towards a place | accusative (chapter 8) |

In this chapter you only need the **Wo?** side: **dative**.

| Preposition | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **in** | in | Das Bett steht **im** (in dem) Schlafzimmer. |
| **an** | on, at (a vertical surface), at the side | Das Bild hängt **an der** Wand. |
| **auf** | on (a horizontal surface) | Das Buch liegt **auf dem** Tisch. |
| **neben** | next to | Die Küche ist **neben dem** Wohnzimmer. |
| **vor** | in front of | Das Auto steht **vor dem** Haus. |
| **hinter** | behind | Der Garten ist **hinter dem** Haus. |
| **über** | above | Die Lampe hängt **über dem** Tisch. |
| **unter** | under | Die Katze schläft **unter dem** Tisch. |
| **zwischen** | between | Der Tisch steht **zwischen den** Stühlen. |

```examples
Die Katze liegt auf dem Sofa. | The cat is lying on the sofa.
Das Kind spielt in der Küche. | The child is playing in the kitchen.
Das Bild ist an der Wand. | The picture is on the wall.
Ich sitze neben ihr. | I am sitting next to her.
Die Kinder spielen vor dem Haus. | The children are playing in front of the house.
Ich sehe den Hirsch zwischen den Bäumen. | I see the deer between the trees.
```

### Contractions

Some prepositions merge with **dem** into one word: **im** (in dem), **am** (an dem), **beim** (bei dem), **zum** (zu dem). With **der**, only *zu* merges (*zur*, from *zu der*); *in der Küche* and *an der Wand* stay in two words.

```achtung
**in der Küche, im Bad, im Wohnzimmer.** Never *in die Küche* when you mean "in the kitchen" (that means moving *into* it). If nothing moves, use the dative: **Wo? → dem / der / den**.
```

## 7.5 stehen, liegen, hängen, sitzen

German is more exact than English about **how** something is positioned. Where English says "is", German often says **stands**, **lies**, **hangs** or **sits**:

| Verb | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **stehen** | upright things: tables, chairs, bottles, cars | Der Tisch **steht** in der Küche. |
| **liegen** | flat things: books, phones, cats, people lying | Das Handy **liegt** auf dem Tisch. |
| **hängen** | things hanging: pictures, lamps, coats | Das Bild **hängt** an der Wand. |
| **sitzen** | people and animals sitting | Anna **sitzt** auf dem Sofa. |

Rule of thumb: ask yourself how the object would look in a drawing. Upright (*stehen*), flat (*liegen*), hanging (*hängen*), seated (*sitzen*).

```examples
Der Schrank steht im Schlafzimmer. | The wardrobe stands in the bedroom.
Das Buch liegt auf dem Sofa. | The book lies on the sofa.
Die Jacke hängt an der Tür. | The jacket hangs on the door.
Der Hund sitzt vor der Tür. | The dog is sitting in front of the door.
```

## 7.6 Living with others

| You say | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Ich wohne **allein**. | I live alone. |
| Ich wohne **mit** meinem Bruder. | I live with my brother. |
| Ich wohne **bei** meinen Eltern. | I live at my parents'. |
| Ich wohne in **einer** WG. | I live in a flat share. |
| Wir wohnen in **einem** Haus. | We live in a house. |
| Meine Wohnung ist **im** dritten Stock. | My flat is on the third floor. |

**mit** and **bei** always take the dative (you will meet more such words in chapter 8): *mit meinem Bruder, bei meinen Eltern*.

## 7.7 Summary

```merke
- A **compound noun** takes the gender of its **last** part: *das Wohnzimmer, der Kühlschrank*.
- **Wo?** is answered by the **dative**: **dem** (m, n), **der** (f), **den** + noun-n (plural); **einem / einer**.
- **an, auf, hinter, in, neben, über, unter, vor, zwischen** take the dative for position.
- **im = in dem, am = an dem, beim = bei dem.**
- Position verbs: **stehen** (upright), **liegen** (flat), **hängen** (hanging), **sitzen** (seated).
- **Kaltmiete + Nebenkosten = Warmmiete.** *Erdgeschoss* is the ground floor.
```

```kultur
**Landeskunde: renting in Germany.** Most people in German cities **rent** (*mieten*) rather than own. A rental contract (*Mietvertrag*) usually requires a **deposit** of up to three months' cold rent. Every house has house rules (*Hausordnung*) with quiet hours (*Ruhezeiten*), usually 22:00 to 6:00 and on Sundays. Rubbish is separated into several bins: paper, plastic, organic waste and the rest. Students often live in a *WG* (*Wohngemeinschaft*), a flat share. On the doorbell you will usually find the family names, not flat numbers.
```
