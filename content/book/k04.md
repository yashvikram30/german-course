## 4.1 Annas Tag

Anna describes an ordinary weekday for her German class. Read it once for the story, then pay attention to the **verbs that split in two** (*steht … auf*, *fängt … an*) and the **time expressions** (*um sieben Uhr*, *am Abend*). Those are the two ideas of this chapter.

```dialogue
title: Mein Tag
setting: Anna reads her text aloud in class.
---
Anna: Ich stehe jeden Tag um sieben Uhr auf. | I get up at seven o'clock every day.
Anna: Um halb acht frühstücke ich: ich esse ein Brötchen und trinke einen Kaffee. | At half past seven I have breakfast: I eat a bread roll and drink a coffee.
Anna: Der Deutschkurs fängt um neun Uhr an und dauert bis zwölf Uhr. | The German course starts at nine o'clock and lasts until twelve.
Anna: Am Nachmittag arbeite ich in einem Café. | In the afternoon I work in a café.
Anna: Am Abend sehe ich oft fern oder ich rufe meine Mutter an. | In the evening I often watch TV or I call my mother.
Anna: Um elf Uhr gehe ich ins Bett und schlafe sofort ein. | At eleven o'clock I go to bed and fall asleep at once.
```

## 4.2 Days, months and seasons

Days, months and seasons are **nouns**, so they are written with a capital letter. All of them are **masculine**: *der Montag, der Juli, der Sommer*. (The only common exception is *das Wochenende*, the weekend.)

| Day | | Month | | Season |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Montag | Monday | Januar | January | der Frühling |
| Dienstag | Tuesday | Februar | February | der Sommer |
| Mittwoch | Wednesday | März | March | der Herbst |
| Donnerstag | Thursday | April | April | der Winter |
| Freitag | Friday | Mai | May | |
| Samstag | Saturday | Juni | June | |
| Sonntag | Sunday | Juli | July | |
| | | August | August | |
| | | September | September | |
| | | Oktober | October | |
| | | November | November | |
| | | Dezember | December | |

```merke
**The week begins on Monday** in German calendars. Saturday is *Samstag* in most of the country, and *Sonnabend* in parts of northern and eastern Germany. In the south you hear *Samstag*.
```

Four little prepositions do almost all the work. **am** (= an dem) and **im** (= in dem) are contractions that use the **dative** (you will meet it properly in chapter 7); for now just learn the fixed phrases. **um** (clock times) and **von … bis** have no article to worry about:

| Use | Preposition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| days and parts of the day | **am** | am Montag, am Morgen, am Abend, am Wochenende |
| months and seasons | **im** | im Januar, im Sommer |
| clock times | **um** | um acht Uhr, um halb zwei |
| a period from … to | **von … bis** | von neun bis zwölf Uhr |

An exception to remember: **in der Nacht** ("at night"), not *am Nacht*, because *die Nacht* is feminine.

```examples
Am Montag habe ich Deutschkurs. | On Monday I have German class.
Im Sommer fahre ich nach Italien. | In summer I go to Italy.
Wir arbeiten von Montag bis Freitag. | We work from Monday to Friday.
Am Wochenende schlafe ich lange. | At the weekend I sleep late.
Mein Geburtstag ist im Mai. | My birthday is in May.
```

Adding **-s** to a day turns it into "on …s" (every Monday): **montags, dienstags**. Parts of the day work the same way: **morgens, mittags, abends, nachts**.

### Today, tomorrow, yesterday

**heute** (today), **morgen** (tomorrow), **gestern** (yesterday). *Morgen* is both "tomorrow" (lower case, adverb) and "morning" (capital, noun): *morgen früh* means "tomorrow morning".

## 4.3 What time is it?

You ask: **Wie spät ist es?** or **Wie viel Uhr ist es?** And **Um wie viel Uhr …?** asks "At what time?".

### The official way (24 hours)

Radio, train stations and timetables use the 24-hour clock: *hours* + **Uhr** + *minutes*.

```examples
Es ist acht Uhr. | It is eight o'clock.
Es ist dreizehn Uhr fünfzehn. | It is 13:15.
Der Zug fährt um achtzehn Uhr dreißig. | The train leaves at 18:30.
Der Film beginnt um zwanzig Uhr. | The film starts at 20:00.
```

### The everyday way (12 hours)

In conversation, Germans use the "past / to" system with **nach** (past) and **vor** (to), plus **Viertel** (quarter) and **halb** (half):

| Clock | Say | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 7:05 | fünf nach sieben | five past seven |
| 7:15 | Viertel nach sieben | quarter past seven |
| 7:25 | fünf vor halb acht | five before half-eight |
| 7:30 | **halb acht** | half past seven |
| 7:35 | fünf nach halb acht | five past half-eight |
| 7:45 | Viertel vor acht | quarter to eight |
| 7:55 | fünf vor acht | five to eight |

```achtung
**halb acht is 7:30, not 8:30.** *Halb* looks forward to the **next** hour: "half (way to) eight". Most learners read *halb acht* as 8:30 because English "half past eight" is 8:30. (British "half seven" means 7:30, but that is a different number from German *halb acht*.) Think "half an hour *before* eight".
```

```tipp
When you do not catch a time, ask **Wie spät ist es?** and then repeat it back with *um*: *Um halb acht, richtig?* ("At half past seven, right?"). It is the quickest way to avoid missing a train.
```

## 4.4 Separable verbs

Many German verbs have a **prefix** that detaches and goes to the **end of the sentence**. These are *trennbare Verben*, "separable verbs". They look like one word in the dictionary (**aufstehen**, to get up) and split when you use them in the present tense:

| | Infinitive | Statement |
|---|---|---|
| aufstehen | to get up | Ich **stehe** um sieben Uhr **auf**. |
| anfangen | to begin | Der Kurs **fängt** um neun Uhr **an**. |
| einkaufen | to shop | Wir **kaufen** am Samstag **ein**. |
| fernsehen | to watch TV | Er **sieht** abends **fern**. |
| anrufen | to phone | Ich **rufe** meine Mutter **an**. |
| mitkommen | to come along | Kommst du **mit**? |

The rule is the same one you already know: the conjugated verb stays in **position 2**, and the **prefix goes to the very end**. The sentence is held together like a bracket, the *Satzklammer*:

> Ich **stehe** jeden Tag um sieben Uhr **auf**.
> verb (2nd position) … … … prefix (last)

The prefix always sits at the end of a statement, however long the sentence becomes. In **yes/no questions** the verb comes first and the prefix is still last: *Stehst du früh auf?*

### How do I know if a prefix separates?

Prefixes that are words on their own, mostly prepositions and adverbs, separate: **an-, auf-, aus-, ein-, mit-, vor-, zu-, ab-, fern-, zurück-**. A second group of prefixes never separates: **be-, ge-, er-, ver-, zer-, ent-**. Those verbs behave like any other verb: *Ich verstehe dich. Ich bekomme einen Brief.*

A useful clue is **stress**: in a separable verb the stress is on the prefix (**AUF**stehen), in an inseparable verb it is on the stem (be**KOM**men).

```examples
Ich stehe früh auf. | I get up early.
Wann fängt der Film an? | When does the film start?
Er ruft seine Eltern an. | He calls his parents.
Kommst du mit? | Are you coming along?
Wir kaufen heute nicht ein. | We are not shopping today.
Ich verstehe das nicht. | I don't understand that. (verstehen is inseparable)
```

## 4.5 Stem-changing verbs

You already met **sprechen** (*du sprichst, er spricht*). Many common verbs change the **vowel of the stem** in two forms only: **du** and **er/sie/es**. Everything else is regular.

| Change | Verb | ich | du | er / sie / es | wir |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| e → i | essen (to eat) | esse | **isst** | **isst** | essen |
| e → i | sprechen | spreche | **sprichst** | **spricht** | sprechen |
| e → ie | lesen (to read) | lese | **liest** | **liest** | lesen |
| e → ie | sehen (to see) | sehe | **siehst** | **sieht** | sehen |
| a → ä | schlafen (to sleep) | schlafe | **schläfst** | **schläft** | schlafen |
| a → ä | fahren (to go by vehicle) | fahre | **fährst** | **fährt** | fahren |
| au → äu | laufen (to run, walk) | laufe | **läufst** | **läuft** | laufen |

Two verbs change more than the vowel: **nehmen** (to take: *ich nehme, du nimmst, er nimmt*) and **treffen** (to meet: *du triffst, er trifft*).

```merke
**The change appears only in the du and er/sie/es forms.** *ich, wir, ihr* and *sie/Sie* keep the infinitive vowel. Dictionaries often list the *er*-form, for example **fahren (fährt)**, so you can work out the *du*-form from it.
```

```examples
Ich esse ein Brötchen, aber Anna isst Müsli. | I eat a bread roll, but Anna eats muesli.
Liest du gern Bücher? | Do you like reading books?
Er schläft acht Stunden. | He sleeps eight hours.
Wir fahren mit dem Bus. | We travel by bus.
Du sprichst sehr gut Deutsch. | You speak German very well.
Am Abend sieht sie fern. | In the evening she watches TV.
```

*Fahren* is the verb for any trip in a vehicle: *Ich fahre nach Berlin* even if it is by train. To "go on foot" use **gehen**.

## 4.6 Putting a sentence together

German likes to give the time first. Because the verb is always second, the subject then moves behind it:

| 1 | verb | subject | rest |
|---|---|---|---|
| Um sieben Uhr | stehe | ich | auf. |
| Am Abend | sehe | ich | fern. |
| Am Samstag | kaufen | wir | ein. |

When several details come together, the usual order is **Time, Manner, Place** (*wann, wie, wo*):

> Ich fahre **morgen** **mit dem Bus** **nach Berlin**.
> time, manner, place

## 4.7 Asking about the day

You now have enough to ask and answer questions about the day:

```examples
Wann stehst du auf? | When do you get up?
Wie viel Uhr ist es? | What time is it?
Um wie viel Uhr fängt der Kurs an? | At what time does the course start?
Was machst du am Wochenende? | What are you doing at the weekend?
Wann hast du Zeit? | When do you have time?
Arbeitest du am Samstag? | Do you work on Saturday?
```

For **how often**, German uses small adverbs, placed after the verb:

| Word | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| immer | always | Ich frühstücke immer um acht. |
| oft | often | Wir sehen oft fern. |
| manchmal | sometimes | Manchmal fahre ich mit dem Rad. |
| nie | never | Ich esse nie Fleisch. |

## 4.8 Summary

```merke
- **Days, months, seasons** are masculine and capitalised. **am** + day or part of the day, **im** + month or season, **um** + clock time.
- **Time:** official = *Uhr* + minutes (*dreizehn Uhr fünfzehn*). Everyday = **nach / vor / Viertel / halb**. **halb acht = 7:30.**
- **Separable verbs** split: conjugated verb in position 2, **prefix at the end**. Prefixes **an-, auf-, aus-, ein-, mit-, fern-, ab-** separate; **be-, ge-, er-, ver-, ent-** do not.
- **Stem-changing verbs** change the vowel only in **du** and **er/sie/es**: *isst, liest, schläft, fährt, sprichst*. **nehmen:** *nimmst, nimmt*.
- **Order:** time, manner, place. Time can come first: the verb stays second.
- **Frequency:** *immer, oft, manchmal, nie.*
```

```kultur
**Landeskunde: punctuality and rest.** Being on time (*pünktlich*) matters in Germany, Austria and Switzerland, for appointments and trains alike. Many shops close on **Sundays** (*Ruhetag*, "day of rest") and noisy activities such as mowing the lawn are discouraged. Opening hours (*Öffnungszeiten*) are posted on the door, for example *Mo–Fr 9–18 Uhr*. Germans write dates as day.month.year: **28.10.2026**.
```
